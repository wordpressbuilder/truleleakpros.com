What Happens During a Water Leak Detection Visit in San Antonio, TX

A typical leak detection visit in San Antonio begins with a pressure isolation test: our technician shuts down each zone of your plumbing and watches a calibrated gauge for pressure drop, confirming whether a leak is active and roughly which branch it occupies. This takes roughly 15 to 30 minutes and immediately narrows the search area before any specialist equipment is deployed.

Electronic and Acoustic Listening

An acoustic leak detector — a ground-contact microphone and amplifier system sensitive enough to hear water escaping through a pinhole — is then placed at intervals along the pipe route. In San Antonio's clay-heavy soil, sound transmission through the ground behaves differently than in sandier terrain, so our network of licensed pros adjust amplifier sensitivity and filter settings accordingly. The equipment isolates the frequency signature of a leak from normal traffic or HVAC noise, narrowing the location to within inches.

Thermal Imaging and Moisture Mapping

An infrared camera scans slab surfaces and interior walls for temperature anomalies caused by escaping water — cold spots under a warm floor slab are a reliable indicator of a slab leak in progress. A moisture meter then confirms elevated readings at the flagged points without requiring a single hole to be cut. Together, these three tools — acoustic leak detector, infrared camera, and moisture meter — give us a documented location that a licensed plumber can act on with a targeted repair rather than speculative demolition.

From arrival to written location report, most San Antonio detection appointments are completed within one to three hours for a standard residential property. Larger foundations or multi-zone commercial plumbing may extend that to a half-day.

How San Antonio's Water System Affects Leak Detection

The San Antonio Water System (SAWS) consistently reports water hardness above 200 mg/L — classified as very hard — across much of its service area. That mineral load is not just a nuisance for appliances; it actively shortens the life of copper pipe by depositing scale that creates pitting and, over years, pinhole corrosion sites that become hidden leaks inside walls and under slabs.

What Hard Water Does to Copper Plumbing

Copper pipe corrodes from the inside out when SAWS water chemistry interacts with aging joints and fittings, particularly in homes built before the 1990s. A water leak detector sensitive enough to find these pinhole leaks must work at very low flow rates — the escaping volume can be as little as a few ounces per minute, invisible without an acoustic or electronic leak detector tuned to the correct frequency band. Our team carries both ultrasonic and ground-contact acoustic sensors precisely because San Antonio pinhole leaks rarely produce the audible drip that tips off a homeowner.

Caliche Soil, Limestone Bedrock, and Buried Lines

Beneath the clay topsoil, much of Bexar County transitions to caliche soil layers and limestone bedrock — a substrate that affects how underground irrigation lines and sewer laterals move during the city's feast-or-famine rainfall cycles. NOAA climate normals show San Antonio averaging roughly 32 inches of rain per year, delivered mostly in intense short bursts rather than steady soaking rain. That rapid saturation followed by fast drying cycles the clay above the caliche repeatedly, stressing buried lines and triggering irrigation leak detection calls throughout the spring and fall. Sewer line repair and drain cleaning are frequently needed after these cycles loosen root intrusions or shift older clay sewer laterals.

Permit-required repairs — including any slab penetration or underground line work — go through the City of San Antonio Development Services Department. Slab Leak Repair Options for San Antonio Homes

Slab leaks are the most common and consequential leak detection call local pros handle in San Antonio, and the city's soil conditions explain exactly why. Expansive Vertisol clay beneath the slab pushes upward during wet seasons and contracts away from the foundation edge during drought — movement that stresses post-tension cable systems and pipe joints in both older and newer homes alike.

Why So Many San Antonio Homes Are Vulnerable

A large share of housing stock across the near North, South, and West sides was built between the 1950s and 1980s with galvanized steel or early copper pipe that is well past its design life. Galvanized pipe corrodes internally, reducing flow and creating leak points that develop slowly inside the slab. Hard freeze events like Winter Storm Uri in February 2021 added another layer of damage: pipes in exterior walls or uninsulated slab chases that had never experienced hard freeze temperatures burst and, in many cases, the resulting slow leaks were not immediately visible — meaning years later, some of those properties are still showing signs of hidden water migration beneath the concrete.

Targeted Repair vs. Full Reroute

Once an acoustic or infrared detection pinpoints the exact leak location, two repair paths are available. A targeted slab penetration repair accesses only the damaged section — this requires permits through the City of San Antonio Development Services Department and is inspected on completion. A full pipe reroute, in which new PEX pipe is run through the attic or interior walls to bypass the slab entirely, eliminates the original copper pipe and avoids future clay-movement stress on buried lines. PEX is now the preferred material for reroutes in San Antonio because its flexibility tolerates soil movement better than rigid copper pipe.

Pool Leak Detection Methods for San Antonio Backyards

Pool leak detection in San Antonio presents a different technical challenge from slab work: the structure is already full of water, the shell may be gunite or fiberglass, and the leak could be in the shell itself, a return line, the skimmer fitting, or the equipment pad plumbing. Our approach separates these possibilities methodically before any excavation is considered.

Pressure Testing vs. Dye Testing

A pressure test isolates the return lines, suction lines, and equipment plumbing separately: each section is capped, pressurized to a set value, and monitored for pressure loss over 15 to 30 minutes. Any line that fails holds the leak. Where shell integrity is in question, a dye test introduces a colored tracer at suspected crack locations or fitting edges — movement of the dye toward the shell surface confirms the site and approximate size of the breach. Pool leak detection using pressure testing combined with dye testing takes roughly two to four hours for a standard Bexar County residential pool.

Acoustic Listening for Underground Pool Lines

Unlike slab leak detection, where the technician listens through concrete, pool line acoustic detection uses equipment pressed against the deck surface above the buried return or suction lines. The acoustic leak detector amplifies the escaping water signature even through a foot of backfill and pool deck. This comparison matters for homeowners choosing between investigation methods: slab leak detection relies heavily on infrared and ground-contact acoustics in tandem, while pool leak detection leans on pressure testing first, with acoustic and dye methods used to confirm and pinpoint. Both services use non-invasive methods as the first step — no excavation, no demo work, until the location is documented.

Gas Leak Detection and Safety Checks in San Antonio

A gas leak in a San Antonio home demands immediate action: evacuate, avoid switching anything electrical on or off, and call from outside. Once the structure is cleared, our team deploys a combustible gas sensor — a catalytic bead or infrared-cell instrument that detects methane and propane concentrations in parts per million — to trace the source along supply lines, appliance connections, and meter risers.

How Gas Leak Detection Works

A gas leak detector is passed slowly along pipe runs at wall penetrations, appliance shutoffs, and flexible connector points. San Antonio's older housing stock means many gas supply lines run through walls that have experienced Winter Storm Uri freeze damage or simply decades of thermal cycling; fittings that were marginal before a hard freeze may now pass gas at trace levels that only a combustible gas sensor can confirm. Where the leak is suspected but not yet located, a pressure decay test on the entire gas system confirms whether the line holds pressure — the same logic as a water pressure isolation test, adapted for gas.

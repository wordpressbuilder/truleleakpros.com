Garland Leak Detection: Slab Leaks in Black Clay Soil Homes

Garland's position on the Blackland Prairie means its residential soils are among the most reactive in the Dallas–Fort Worth region, and that reactivity directly determines how often slab leaks occur here. When drought conditions classified by the US Drought Monitor as D2 or higher set in — which happens periodically across the Dallas area — the clay beneath a slab can shrink unevenly, pulling away from foundation edges and creating differential movement that opens micro-cracks in pipes that may have passed a recent inspection without fault.

A significant share of Garland's housing stock was constructed between the 1950s and 1980s, when embedding copper pipe directly in concrete was standard practice. Those pipes are now 40 to 70 years past original construction — well within or beyond the statistical service life of that installation method. Homes from this era may also contain polybutylene pipe, which was widely used as a copper replacement through the late 1980s and is known to degrade from the inside out, making external visual inspection useless and acoustic or electronic detection essential.

Our electronic leak detection process under a Garland slab follows a structured sequence. First, we isolate the supply line and use a digital pressure gauge to confirm and measure loss. We then introduce tracer gas — typically a hydrogen-nitrogen blend — into the pressurised pipe and deploy a surface-sensitive gas detector to map where the tracer escapes through the slab. Acoustic correlation equipment cross-references that signal by listening for the characteristic frequency signature of water escaping under pressure through concrete. Thermal imaging of the slab surface adds a third data layer, identifying temperature differentials caused by cooler escaping water. The intersection of all three data points gives us a precise location, limiting excavation to the smallest possible area.

Beyond slab leak detection, our work in Garland regularly includes drain cleaning for clogged lines stressed by root intrusion and soil movement, and sewer line repair for cast-iron drain stacks common in the city's older homes. Those services often surface during the same inspection that confirms a slab leak, so we coordinate them together where possible.

How North Texas Municipal Water District Billing Reveals Hidden Leaks

Water pressure inside Plano supply lines also matters for ongoing pipe health. Pool Leak Detection for Plano Backyard Pools

Pool leak detection for Plano backyard pools is complicated by one factor that does not apply in every Texas city: the same Blackland Prairie clay soil that moves under slab foundations also moves under pool shells and underground return lines. A pool that appears to be losing water through evaporation may actually be losing it through a pressure-side return line where the soil has shifted and separated a joint. Standard evaporation tests — the bucket test — will not distinguish the two.

Our pool leak detection method uses dye testing at suspected fault points (skimmer throats, return fittings, light niches, and step joints) combined with pressure testing of underground plumbing runs. Dye injection shows active suction or outflow at a specific location visually, without disturbing the pool finish or surrounding hardscape — an approach that works within HOA access and aesthetic requirements that many Plano backyard developments impose. If dye confirms a structural crack or fitting failure, pressure testing of the underground return and suction lines identifies whether the loss is at the shell or beneath it.

A pool leak that runs for a full billing cycle adds measurable volume loss to a Plano property's water meter reading. Where pool loss is suspected, we cross-reference metre readings against pool capacity and estimated evaporation rates to give homeowners a documented case for further investigation.

Spot Repair Versus Full Re-Route

A spot repair — cutting one precise opening in the concrete, fixing the failed section of copper pipe and sealing the slab — costs less upfront and makes sense when acoustic leak detection equipment and a pressure test confirm a single isolated failure on a line that is otherwise structurally sound. For a newer home in a master-planned development such as Stonebridge Ranch or Craig Ranch, where only a small fitting was improperly torqued during high-volume construction, a spot repair is often the right call.

A full re-route runs PEX pipe above the slab through walls and ceilings, bypassing the existing embedded line entirely. It costs more initially but eliminates the need for repeated concrete cuts if the foundation continues to move — which it will, given McKinney's clay geology. When a pressure test reveals pressure loss across a long run of copper pipe, or when thermal imaging shows multiple warm spots in the flooring, re-routing is the more cost-effective long-term decision for a McKinney foundation.

What Pushes the Price Higher

Post-reroute permit fees through City of McKinney Development Services, the thickness of the concrete slab, the location of the failed line under a load-bearing section, and the need for flooring restoration all add to the final total. Permits for any repair that involves opening a slab or touching a water service line must be pulled and inspected through the city's online portal, so accurate pre-cut pinpointing is not optional — an unnecessary opening creates an extra permit event and extends the timeline. Scheduling your detection and any repair work before September, when demand for slab leak repair in McKinney peaks alongside late-summer soil shrinkage, helps avoid extended lead times.

Why McKinney's Expansive Clay Soil Leads to Slab Leaks

McKinney sits on Blackland Prairie geology — one of the most reactive expansive clay formations in Texas, classified by Texas A&M AgriLife Extension as a soil type that swells significantly when wet and contracts sharply during drought. That seasonal cycling exerts lateral and vertical pressure on every slab-on-grade foundation in the city, and the copper or PEX supply lines embedded within those slabs flex with every movement.

How Clay Movement Stresses Embedded Plumbing

Unlike sandy-soil regions where foundations remain relatively stable, McKinney slabs can experience measurable vertical displacement between a wet spring and a dry August. That movement bends embedded pipes at their fittings — often repeatedly — until a micro-fracture forms. Because the fracture sits inside concrete, there is no visible surface sign; the only early indicators are a rising water meter reading or warm spots in a tile floor above a hot-water line.

This is why non-invasive acoustic leak detection equipment is essential before any excavation decision in McKinney. Spotting a Pool Leak in Your McKinney Backyard

A pool losing more than a quarter-inch of water per day after accounting for evaporation likely has a pool leak — either in the shell, the return or suction plumbing, or at a fitting. McKinney's hot summers accelerate evaporation enough to mask early-stage leaks, so the bucket test is the starting point: place a filled bucket on a pool step, mark both the bucket water level and the pool water level, and compare the two after 24 hours with the pump off.

How We Confirm and Locate the Leak

If the pool loses water faster than the bucket, our team moves to a pressure test of the underground return and suction lines, isolating each circuit to identify where pressure drops. A moisture meter scanned along the pool deck and surrounding soil pinpoints where saturated ground indicates a leaking lateral. For shell cracks, dye testing at suspected fittings reveals flow direction and confirms the source without draining the pool unnecessarily.

Collin County's flash flooding risk means pool surroundings can be waterlogged after a storm — a condition that temporarily masks pool leak symptoms by equalising the soil moisture around pipe joints. We account for recent rainfall in our diagnostic sequencing, performing pressure tests first so soil saturation does not produce a false negative on the moisture meter scan.

Pipe Material and Repair Complexity

Checking a Pool Leak in an Arlington Backyard

Pool leak detection in Arlington backyards starts with ruling out evaporation before any equipment is deployed — in Arlington's high-heat summers, an uncovered pool can lose an inch or more of water per week to evaporation alone. The standard bucket test (filling a container to pool water level, setting it on a step, and comparing loss rates over 24 hours) distinguishes evaporation from a structural or plumbing leak before we commit to invasive testing.

When the bucket test confirms a real leak, our team runs a water pressure test on the return lines, skimmer lines, and main drain plumbing to identify any section holding pressure below specification. We follow that with an acoustic leak detector sweep along buried lateral lines, listening for the turbulent flow signature that escapes through a cracked return fitting or a joint failure in the underground plumbing. Pool plumbing in Arlington's clay soil is subject to the same ground movement stresses as residential slab pipes, so lateral line joint failures are a frequent finding rather than an unusual one.

Arlington's high summer irrigation demand also means that an undetected pool leak and an undetected irrigation line leak can compound on the same monthly water bill, making it difficult for homeowners to attribute the spike to a single source. Local pros test both systems independently when both are suspected, using zone-by-zone isolation to identify which line is losing pressure.
